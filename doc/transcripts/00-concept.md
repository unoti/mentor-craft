## GPT-5
I love the concept, and I think it makes a ton of sense. You're basically creating a kind of training pipeline where each iteration, each little “day” of working with the agent, turns into a training example. And over time, you just accumulate this library of fine-tuning pairs. On one side, you’ve got the vague initial feature request or rough concept, and on the other side, you’ve got the refined design and even the implemented code. That’s a pretty neat way to build a custom-tailored model.

Now, in terms of how much you can do with something like LoRA, or low-rank adaptation, it’s actually quite efficient for this kind of iterative, smaller-scale fine-tuning. LoRA is designed to add a lightweight set of trainable parameters on top of a base model, so you’re not re-training the whole LLM from scratch. Instead, you’re just nudging it in the direction of your style and your domain knowledge.

In practice, that can actually work really well, especially if you have a decent number of examples. If you have a bunch of these pairs and you keep adding to them over time, LoRA can be a pretty good fit. It’s a lot less resource-intensive than full retraining, and you can do it more frequently.

Of course, if you find that you need a larger scope of adaptation, then a full fine-tuning might eventually be on the table. But I’d say starting with LoRA is a great first step. It lets you iterate quickly and see if the agent really does get better at reflecting your unique design patterns and domain quirks. So yeah, I think that sounds like a really solid approach.
